## Overview

This section of the website documents the manual installation of **The Phoenix Flavour**. For more information about the setup itself, its philosophy and the mods it contains, please refer to [this page].

### Aim and Purpose

The Phoenix Flavour is intended principally as a **learning resource** for modding beginners. No previous knowledge is required to follow my instructions. Throughout the guide, I will explain various aspects of modding so that you understand not only *what* to do but *why*. As such, TPF is a great starting point for new modders.

> Please note that following TPF is not the same thing as building your own setup. The time-intensive process of mod selection and nearly all of the patching as well as the mod and load order are provided for you. Nonetheless, the purpose of the guide is to equip you with much of the knowledge required to do these things on your own later on.

**But TPF is not only a resource for beginners**. I have assembled a vast collection of mods with additional notes and instructions for further modifications that you can also draw on for your personal setup. Likewise, my instructions for properly setting up Skyrim SE and the various modding tools should come in handy as the foundation for any custom modlist. Even if you do not intend to follow the guide in its entirety, you are welcome to refer to my instructions and recommendations for inspiration.

### Structure

The process of installing TPF is split into three phases:

1. **Initial Setup:** Setup of the game and workspace (installation of modding tools, INI configuration, etc).
2. **Mod Installation:** Installation, modification, configuration of all mods. This is by far the most time-intensive part.
3. **Finalisation:** Running automated patchers (including LOD generation), generating facegen, and setting up MCMs.

This three-phase process is applicable to the construction of any modded setup.

### Customisation

Customisability is key to the appeal of moddding in general. Unfortunately, a big part of pre-configured setups -- modding guides or automated installers (Collections, Wabbajack) -- is that nearly all choices have been made for you. TPF is no exception to this rule. **If you decide to follow the guide, you should follow it to the letter.** Many instructions depend on each other and many mods do, too. Please understand that I can offer no support to users who deviate from the guide.

However, in this present iteration of TPF I have tried to incorporate as many choices as possible without massively increasing the guide's complexity. While mods tend to interact with each other due to the way they are structured (more on that later), many mods are properly standalone and can be included or excluded based on user preference. **Various mods in this guide are marked as "optional" and can be skipped.** I have also indicated my own preferences, usually with an explanation.

An advantage of this approach in TPF is that **performance is customisable**. Unlike auto-installed setups, TPF allows you to fine-tune the setup's performance impact by allowing you to pick smaller textures, skip high-poly meshes, reduce LOD settings, and more. This should make TPF usable on basically any PC that can run vanilla Skyrim while also scaling up to give high-end systems a proper workout.

### Additional Guides

In addition to the core guide, which provides the instructions for setting up TPF, I have written various other short(ish) articles that explain aspects of modding Skyrim. These articles are supplementary and not necessary for following the guide unless otherwise noted. They are provided to expand your (technical) knowledge of mods and should help you further customise TPF or build your own setup.

### A Personal Note

TPF represents not only my taste in mods but also my approach to modding. I have spent well over a decade developing and refining that approach, and of course it is still changing when new tools are released or I figure out better ways of doing things. Generally speaking, I am very neat and structured in how I work, and TPF contains many instructions which reflect that.

However, let me make one thing clear: **I do not claim that my approach to modding is the best one**, and it certainly isn't the only one. Throughout the guide, I have done my best to give you a head's up when instructions are not strictly necessary; these are then marked as optional and I explain why I have included them nevertheless. If you appreciate a very structured approach, you should have a great time with TPF -- but you can also choose to be less thorough and skip the optional stuff, and that's perfectly fine, too!
